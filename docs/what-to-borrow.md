# What to borrow, and from what

**Status: research. Written by Claude on 2026-09-08, to be checked and owned by
the project lead.** Sources are named. Where a figure could not be confirmed at
its origin, it says so and should not be used until it is.

---

## The reframe

**The competitor is not Multi Me, or Nourish, or any of the thirty-one
conformant systems. It is paper — and paper is winning by orders of
magnitude.**

Everything below is person-held, adopted at scale, and made of card. All of it
works. None of it has an operator, because paper does not need one. Each fails
in exactly one way, and it is always the same way: **it does not travel.** It
is left at home, lost in a transfer, or copied out by hand at every boundary.

That gives the project a much better opening sentence than decentralisation
does:

> Keep what paper already gets right — nobody holds it, nobody can lose it
> centrally, no company can stop trading — and fix the one thing it cannot do.

It also puts a very large number of people on the same side of the argument as
this project, rather than in the way of it.

## 1. "This is me" — Alzheimer's Society

**The most important comparator, and the direct ancestor of the About Me
standard this project is scoped to.**

Verified from the Alzheimer's Society's own page:

- **Fourth edition**, and "endorsed by the Royal College of Nursing since
  2010."
- For "anyone receiving professional care who is living with dementia or
  experiencing delirium **or other communication difficulties**."

That last phrase matters for reach. It is not a learning-disability tool and
not strictly a dementia one: it is aimed at anybody who cannot easily say these
things for themselves, which is the same population this project is for and a
far broader one than Multi Me's.

> ⚠️ **A widely repeated figure of "more than 840,000" people could not be
> confirmed.** It appears in search results attributed to the Alzheimer's
> Society, but it is **not on their own "This is me" page**, and the blog post
> that may carry it returned 403. **Do not use this number in a funding
> application or a meeting until it has been verified at source.** If it is
> right it is a powerful number; being caught using an unverified one is worse
> than not having it.

### What to borrow: the words

Fifteen years and four editions of refinement by people using it in real
hospitals. The PRSB headings this project uses descend from this work, but the
*prompts underneath the headings* are where the craft is, and they were shaped
by watching people struggle to fill them in.

**Read the actual leaflet and take the phrasing.** Inventing wording that has
already been tested on hundreds of thousands of people is work for its own
sake.

## 2. Message in a Bottle — Lions Club

**The idea worth stealing is not the bottle.**

The scheme: personal and medical details on a standard form, kept in a bottle,
kept **in the fridge** — always the fridge, everywhere, by agreement. Two green
cross stickers, **one on the front door and one on the fridge door**. Bottles
are free from chemists, GP surgeries, police stations and housing associations.

So a paramedic who has never heard of the scheme walks into a stranger's home,
sees a sticker, and learns two things at once: **that a record exists, and
exactly where to find it.**

### What to borrow: a discovery protocol

**This project has no equivalent, and that is a real gap.**

Hearth is good once somebody is in the circle. Nothing tells a paramedic who
has never heard of Hearth that it exists at all. The record could be perfect
and still never be read, which is the failure that matters most — the moment
it is needed is usually a moment nobody planned.

Paper solved this with a sticker. The digital equivalent is an open question
and worth treating as a design problem in its own right, not an afterthought:

- something physical in the home, as the bottle scheme has
- something on a phone's lock screen, where emergency information already lives
- something a professional could be told to look for once, and would then
  recognise everywhere

None of it is built. It is written down here because "how is it found" is a
different problem from "what does it hold", and this project has only solved
the second.

## 3. The Herbert Protocol — police forces, with Dementia UK and others

A form completed **in advance** about a person with dementia — places of
importance to them, places they frequent, health issues, where they have been
found before. Kept at home or with the care home. **If the person goes missing,
it is handed to the police**, who can start searching immediately instead of
asking a distressed family to remember.

Hosted by police forces themselves — Police Scotland, GMP, PSNI, the Met — as a
downloadable blank.

### Two things to borrow

**Filled in while calm, used while panicking.** The writing and the reading
happen in completely different states of mind. This project already half knows
this — the record is written unhurried and read by somebody with a clipboard —
but it is worth holding explicitly when designing anything.

**An institution can distribute a blank without becoming the operator of
anybody's data.** Police forces host the form; they do not hold the completed
ones. That is a distribution model directly relevant to the NHS route, where
the whole difficulty is that nobody wants to hold the parcel. Somebody hosting
a download is not somebody holding a record.

## What this changes about the pitch

Not "a decentralised care record". Something closer to:

> Hundreds of thousands of families already keep this on paper, because
> nothing else works when the person cannot speak for themselves. Paper gets
> the important part right: no company holds it and no company can lose it. It
> just cannot follow somebody from home to hospital to a new care agency. That
> is the part worth fixing, and it is the only part.

Two things it earns:

- **It stops being a technology pitch.** Holochain becomes how, not what,
  which is the right order for anybody who does not already care about
  peer-to-peer.
- **It answers "why hasn't somebody done this?"** — somebody has, hundreds of
  thousands of times, on card. That is evidence the need is real rather than a
  reason the idea is taken.
